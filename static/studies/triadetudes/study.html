Chords take the bar's slots in order — e.g. 5/4 split 2+3: first chord 2 beats, next chord 3, new bar. If the metronome is running, Play joins it at the next bar — the click you already hear is your count-in. If it isn't, Play starts it (count-in adds one clicked bar first). Stopping the étude leaves a self-started metronome running; the metronome's Stop stops everything — it owns the clock. Each slider's speaker icon mutes that voice — the icon is the slider at zero, and a muted chord with bass and click sounding is play-along: you supply the chords. The click's own level lives in the Metronome card — the metronome owns its sound.

Centricity

Triads first, meaning added underneath: with a bass, every dot is relabeled relative to the new root — the same shape changes meaning.
Type the progression you actually face, or grow it chip by chip with +. The app derives the upper-structure triad on your string set and grounds it on the chord's root in the bass. Tap a chip to edit it — where a chord holds more than one honest triad the chip grows a ▾. Romans resolve in the selected key.

Shape & Motion

? pivots, placement, and the badges Pivots: click any scale note on a string of the set — that note and the next two scale notes up the same string become your pivots (dashed rings). Placement decides where the figure's notes live: Grip — one note per string, playable as a chord, anchored to your pivots; Line — notes placed freely along the set, up to three per string, preferring the pivot window, playable in sequence rather than as a chord; Free — the grip chosen by smoothest voice-leading, anchor released. The dashed box simply draws where the figure ended up living — a consequence of the placement, not a setting. Changing set, key or scale carries your pattern and pivots along — same figure, same degrees, new context. Order badges on the sounding notes show each string's position(s) in the pattern.

Triadetudes v0.8.14 — triad isolation étude designer. Colors per At-Etudes Design Spec v1.1
(red R · green 2 · blue 3 · silver 4 · black 5 · cyan 6 · amber 7; function relative to the
sounding root). The score shows the whole étude; the arpeggio pattern's note count against each
chord's beats sets the subdivision (4 notes over 2 beats = 8ths). Audio is synthesized; click any
note or key to hear it. The practice journal saves each étude's full configuration with one
note — history lives in this browser; Export writes one .atchart.md any At-Etudes app can
open.
